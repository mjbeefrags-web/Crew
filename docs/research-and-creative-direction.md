# Research & Creative Direction

> Historical M1.1 research covers the provisional ARCFIELD exploration. The active storefront identity is now Crewww, based on the user-supplied product references in `docs/material/`; this document is retained as history and does not define the current brand.

## Research method

Primary research used official retailer pages retrieved on 22 Nov 2026 through page text retrieval and direct Chromium navigation. “Observed” means the page content was actually accessible; links alone do not count as evidence. Browser blocks, errors, redirects, and maintenance pages are recorded as such. No competitor layouts, copy, or assets are used in ARCFIELD.

Secondary sources at the end support general usability and implementation principles. They do **not** substitute for brand-specific observations. Where a retailer was inaccessible, no claim is made about its current navigation, product UX, checkout, or motion.

### Research completeness

Direct primary evidence is strongest for Nike, SSENSE (limited), Gymshark, and Streetwearts. Seven fashion houses/retailers were blocked, errored, or in maintenance. This audit supplements them with independent, general UX research rather than inventing brand findings. A human competitor review remains useful before visual creative lock, especially for current campaign and navigation patterns.

## Secondary research and technical sources

- Nielsen Norman Group, [Mega Menus Work Well for Site Navigation](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mega-menus-work-well/) (2017): structured two-dimensional groups can expose many choices; labels and grouping must remain easy to scan. Use it to inform a taxonomy-first mega-menu, not as evidence about any named fashion brand.
- Nielsen Norman Group, [Basic Patterns for Mobile Navigation](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-navigation-patterns/) (2015): navigation and search compete with content for mobile screen space; both should remain discoverable without crowding the page.
- Nielsen Norman Group, [Memory Recognition and Recall in User Interfaces](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/recognition-and-recall/) (2024): visible choices reduce reliance on remembering exact queries/labels; supports browseable denim-fit and category routes alongside search.
- Baymard Institute, [Checkout Optimization: 5 Ways to Minimize Form Fields in Checkout](https://baymard.com/research-articles/checkout-flow-average-form-fields) (2024): argues field burden is more meaningful than merely minimizing the number of steps. Keep checkout concise and ask only necessary information. The article page was accessible; its underlying premium benchmark is not reproduced here.
- W3C, [WCAG 2.2 Target Size (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html): 24×24 CSS px minimum with defined exceptions; use larger touch targets where feasible.
- W3C, [WCAG 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html): interaction-triggered nonessential motion should be disableable (AAA); reduced-motion support is a design requirement for this motion-heavy brief.
- Motion, [Accessibility](https://motion.dev/docs/react-accessibility): reduce large transforms/parallax and autoplay under reduced motion.
- GSAP, [ScrollTrigger](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/): scroll-linked scrub/pin/trigger capabilities; pinning and scrubbing should be reserved for authored scenes, not routine navigation.
- React Three Fiber, [Scaling Performance](https://r3f.docs.pmnd.rs/advanced/scaling-performance): on-demand rendering and resource reuse reduce render/battery costs.
- web.dev, [Optimize Cumulative Layout Shift](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-cls): images/embeds without reserved dimensions and late content can cause layout shifts; reserve media geometry.

These are secondary, general principles. They do not claim that any blocked retailer implements a particular pattern.

## Brand foundation reassessment

### ARCFIELD: keep as a working name, not a locked identity

**Fit:** ARCFIELD is distinctive, concise, and visually workable. “Field” and “arc” naturally cue outdoors, technical performance, and movement. That was a useful foundation exploration, but it over-indexes on performance apparel for a business that must cover adult clothing, denim, kids, drops, accessories and shoes.

**Recommendation:** retain ARCFIELD only as a provisional project name pending client/name/trademark/domain and local-language review. Reposition away from sportswear. A brand name that better signals contemporary local fashion may ultimately fit better, but no replacement should be invented without client input.

### Positioning and voice

- Move from **“Built for effort. Made for living.”** to a broader working proposition: **contemporary clothes with character, made for real everyday wardrobes.** This is a strategy statement, not approved customer-facing copy.
- Primary audience: local, style-aware young adults seeking expressive, wearable fashion and clear fit/value information; the range also serves kids/families as a distinct shopping audience.
- Keep the voice confident, youthful and editorial, but practical when describing fit, sizing, stock, delivery, returns and stores. Avoid performance clichés and unsupported local/sustainability claims.
- The client’s exact home market, language, currency, customer age bands and commercial policies require confirmation. Streetwearts is an Egyptian reference; it does not prove that the client operates in Egypt.

### Visual system assessment

- **Keep as a base:** warm paper and graphite provide a restrained editorial canvas; the serif/neutral-sans contrast is premium and visually distinct.
- **Reduce:** deep pine, lichen, field coordinates, “field tested,” technical mono labels, abstract orbits and garment-as-sculpture currently read outdoor/performance. Keep these as occasional campaign motifs rather than the whole brand vocabulary.
- **Evolve:** introduce a more confident contemporary grotesk for category/product navigation, use serif selectively for campaign statements, and reserve mono for genuinely technical metadata. The current mobile serif headline consumes substantial screen height; future pages need tighter responsive type ratios.
- **Color:** retain neutral paper/ink as a flexible base; make the accent collection-led and more expressive. Do not lock a generic “fashion red” before the client’s actual product palette is known.
- **Image:** use local casting, recognizable places, and product-on-body plus detail imagery only with licensed/commissioned rights. Product accuracy and fit remain more important than dramatic treatment.
- **Language/localization:** build locale and currency as data/config, not hardcoded copy. If the target market is Egypt, test Arabic typography, RTL layout, EGP formatting, delivery/payment expectations, and contact conventions with the client.

## Creative direction for the eventual experience

Build a fashion-led, local-first editorial world where product discovery stays one interaction away. The campaign should have a clear seasonal point of view; color and imagery can shift per Drop while typography, product information, navigation and checkout remain consistent. Do not make a decorative 3D scene the identity. Tie each interactive image, 3D moment and transition to a product, collection or story with a non-WebGL route that works just as well.
